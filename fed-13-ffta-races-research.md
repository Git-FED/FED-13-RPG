## Sub-races and divisions, and what carries over

### Drakescale (Bangaa): the four sub-races carry over, renamed

FFTA Bangaa divide into four biological sub-branches by scale color and ear length:

- Ruga: yellow-brown scales, shorter ears, gentle scholars, monks, instructors, hereditary priests and lawmakers.
- Faas: bronze scales, heavy athletic builds, frontline warriors, "Faas" means soldier or a strong sense of justice, they enforce Bangaa law alongside the Ruga.
- Bista: copper scales, relaxed and easygoing, integrate smoothly into Hume-dominated cities.
- Sanga: ash-gray scales, laid-back, live alongside Humes in urban trade hubs.

FED-13 decision: the four sub-races carry over with the lore above, under the names already decided in the design: Ruga is Solwrights, Faas is Bronzeguard, Bista is Emberfolk, Sanga is Ashenmoor. The renamed names stay. They are already in new-game.md line 85, in new-game-bondkin-advancement.md, in fed-13-rpg-readme.md, and in character names (Draven Ashenmoor). Reverting to the FFTA originals would undo a decided rename and break those names. The paste's value here is the lore, which maps 1 to 1 onto the renamed versions.

Other Bangaa facts that carry over: Bangaa live 1.5 to 2 times longer than Humes. They are warm-blooded mammals who give live birth, not egg layers, and calling one a lizard is a severe insult. Older Bangaa show age through long braided facial hair and dulled or scarred scales, not wrinkles. Respected leaders carry a two-letter honorific prefix with an apostrophe, such as Ba'Gamnan. In FED-13, a Drakescale elder or leader may carry a Ba' style prefix as an honorific, never as a name change. Bangaa have a mountain stronghold town in FFTA called Sprohm. In FED-13 the Drakescale stronghold is Stonehold, already named in new-game-bondkin-advancement.md.

### Harefolk (Viera): two sub-races carry over, Feol stays dropped

FFTA Viera divide into three sub-races plus a cultural exile rule:

- Rava: dark-skinned, dense sun-dappled forests like the Golmore Jungle, strictly isolationist villages.
- Veena: fair-skinned, pale silver-white hair, high-altitude mountain forests like the Skatay Range, isolated matriarchal villages guarded by unseen male Wood-warders.
- Feol: pale-skinned, short-eared half-Viera, descended from an ancient union with the Aegyl leader Feolthanos, blonde hair, longer lifespans, shunned as outcasts.

FED-13 decision: Rava and Veena carry over under the names already decided in the design: Rava is Deepbark, Veena is Snowpine. Feol stays dropped, already decided in fed-13-monster-legal-renames.md, and the Palebranch Exiles already replace it. The renamed names stay, for the same reason as the Drakescale names: they are already in new-game.md line 85, in the advancement file, and in character names (Lina Palebranch).

The Green Word exile rule carries over: a Harefolk who leaves the home village loses the voice of the wood, discards the village surname, and is permanently exiled from the birth clan. In FED-13 the Green Word is called the Wood Law, and a male protector is called a Woodwarden, already decided in new-game-bondkin-advancement.md. The Palebranch Exiles are Harefolk who broke the Wood Law and live outside the villages.

Other Viera facts that carry over: about eight in ten Viera born are women. Male and female kits are physically indistinguishable until around age 13, when males are taken by solitary Wood-warders to train in the deep forest. Viera villages are matriarchal.

### Elderkin (Nu Mou): the four types already decided, divisions slot into them

Elderkin is Nu Mou renamed. The four types are already decided in fed-13-elderkin-nu-mou-races.md: Dream (FFTA), Original (FFXII), Spirit (FFXIV), Jylland (FFTA2). The paste's Nu Mou divisions slot into those four types:

- Kiltias Sect: Nu Mou acolytes atop Mt. Bur-Omisace, monks, healers, caretakers for pilgrims and war wounded, guardians of ancient magic and shrines. FED-13: the Kiltias Sect is an Original Elderkin order, based at Mount Veylspire.
- Scholarly orders and enclaves: Cadoan in FFTA, the premier center of higher learning, magic research, and alchemy; Moorabella in FFTA2. FED-13: the scholarly orders are Cairnwick and The Quiet Flame Order, already named in new-game.md line 85, plus Mount Veylspire.
- Rare clans: Nu Mou Nobles led by the Arcanist Madreth in the Rupie Mountains, an organized territorial clan that wars with rival Bangaa clans; the Duelhorn syndicate, co-led by Duke Snakeheart. FED-13: these are Jylland Elderkin clans, mercenary guilds and syndicates competing for territory.
- Fae variant: in FFXIV, Nu Mou are fae creatures in Il Mheg, not mortal citizens, divided strictly by contractual duty and an unbreakable code of equal exchange. FED-13: the Fae variant is the Spirit Elderkin type, with the Artisans of Pla Enni and the Lawless Ones as its two factions.

Elderkin young title stays as already decided: Apprentice, Child, Young Scholar. Do not use Pup or Kit. Elder title stays: Elder, Master Scholar, High Elder, Master Acolyte. Elderkin lifespan stays: more than 3 times Humes, roughly 200 to 250 years. Elderkin shun water and avoid it at all cost.

### Hybrid (Hume): divided by nation, caste, and bloodline

Humes have no biological sub-races. The paste shows they divide by empire and nation, by social caste and birthright, and by magical bloodline. FED-13 decision: the three division layers become the organizing principle of the Hybrid clans already named in new-game.md line 54:

- By nation: Kingdom of Caldara, March of Ostara.
- By caste and house: House Corvane, House Draeven, Highspire.
- By magical bloodline: Clan Thornwhistle, the Dynast-bloodline clan, descended from an ancient ruling line able to wield high magic and bind Totem Spirits.

A Hybrid changes clan like a job change, never bloodline or species. Hybrid lifespan is the baseline other bloodlines are measured against.

### Puffkin (Moogle): guilds and ear variants

Moogles have no named biological sub-races in Ivalice. The paste divides them by region and by engineering and mercantile guilds: Baguba Port airship hub, Clan Centurio hunter clan, airship workshops. FED-13 decision: the Puffkin guilds already named in new-game.md line 85 stay: Sky-ear (airship crews), Stone-ear (workshop engineers), Galeport (merchants), Clan Brightpom (hunter clan), Tinkerwright Guild (master engineers). The regional variant idea becomes a visual variant only: Puffkin have two ear shapes, long rabbit ears (Ivalice) and round bear ears (classic), which changes appearance only, never stats or jobs.

### The other nine bloodlines: divided by trade, clan, or hive

The paste gives no sub-races for Seeq, Gria, Aegyl, Garif, Revgaji, Baknamy, Urutan-Yensa, Occuria, or Helgas. FED-13 decision: none of the nine has a biological sub-race. Each divides by trade, clan, or home instead:

- Mudsnout (Seeq): no sub-races. Divides by work crew: mercenary bands, dock gangs, labor crews.
- Wyrmwing (Gria): no sub-races. Divides by aerial clan, one sky rookery per clan.
- Skyborn (Aegyl): no sub-races. Divides by sky island, one floating island per clan. Short lifespans, already in the paste.
- Furmask (Garif): no sub-races. Divides by caravan, one masked caravan per clan, mask pattern marks the caravan.
- Pardkin (Revgaji): no sub-races. Divides by trade house: merchant houses, scholar circles, auction houses.
- Ashmask (Baknamy): no sub-races. Divides by cave warren, one warren per clan.
- Dunehusk (Urutan-Yensa): no sub-races. Divides by sandsea hive, one sandsea per hive.
- Fatewardens (Occuria): no sub-races, no divisions. One order, the Wardens, who speak as one.
- Helgas: no sub-races. The few Helgas left are the last of one line.

## Spices and food mission items

From the paste: FFTA has no item category called spices, but several food and ingredient Mission Items drive pub missions: Eldagusto (rare seasoning, link-cable Reserve Missions, Sauce Recipe A or B), Gedegg Soup, Choco Bread, Grownup Bread, Kiddy Bread, Choco Gratin, Jerky, Malboro Wine, Blood Apple, Magic Fruit, Power Fruit, Gysahl Greens, Malboro Spice (FFTA2), Hot Recipe mission #047 (Bomb Shells from Bomb monsters for a super-spicy dish), Herb Picking mission #001 (Muscomulai herbs from Giza Plains). FED-13 decisions:

- Eldagusto is a distinctive FFTA name, so it is renamed. FED-13 name: Elderspice, a rare seasoning. It counts as rare spice in recipes, so it is the named rare spice for Grand Feast. It comes from harvesting rare finds and from the Hot Recipe mission.
- Malboro Spice is renamed, since Malboro is a Final Fantasy monster name. FED-13 name: Mire Spice, a hot swamp spice. It counts as fire spice in recipes, so it is the named fire spice for Ember Skewers. It comes from harvesting in swamp and marsh areas.
- Hot Recipe becomes a FED-13 guild cooking mission: bring Bomb Shells dropped by Bomb Beasts to the Saltquay chef. Reward: Elderspice x3 and 500 Cr. Repeatable.
- Herb Picking becomes a FED-13 guild gathering mission: gather Muskroot, a medicinal herb, in the Realm Map fields. Muscomulai is renamed Muskroot. This is the first gathering mission Guildmaster Bram Hollis posts after Trial Battle 1, alongside the logging run already in the design.
- Gysahl Greens is renamed, since it is tied to Chocobo. FED-13 name: Strider Greens, feed for Golden Strider mounts. Gathered by harvesting.
- Blood Apple, Magic Fruit, and Power Fruit become three Grovefruit types within the 21: Bloodapple Grovefruit (restores HP in battle), Magicfruit Grovefruit (restores MP in battle), Powerfruit Grovefruit (raises attack for the next battle). They grow on grove trees, sell at Puffkin shops, and come from harvesting missions like other Grovefruit.
- The prepared foods become Puffkin shop food items and guild delivery mission items, not cooked dishes. The six cooked dishes in new-game.md line 362 stay unchanged. Renames: Gedegg Soup becomes Golden Egg Soup, Choco Bread becomes Strider Bread, Grownup Bread becomes Trail Bread, Kiddy Bread becomes Kiddy Rolls, Choco Gratin becomes Strider Gratin, Malboro Wine becomes Mire Wine, Jerky stays Jerky. Each restores 25 HP out of battle and sells for 50 Cr. Guild delivery missions ask the trainer to deliver them to clients across the 13 worlds.

## How Bondkin join: they ask, you never hunt them

Git-FED's rule, 2026-11-23: Bondkin are NOT found or hunted down in the wild. Catching is for Beasts and Feral only. Bondkin join by ASKING to join your team, the FFTA way, where characters come up and ask to join you. You do not need to go find them like monsters. Dispatch mission returns with a new teammate is one way this happens, but the general rule is: Bondkin ask to join, you do not hunt them.

FED-13 decision: this is a core rule, stated in new-game.md. The four ways a Bondkin joins:

1. Starter choice: the 5 core bloodlines (Hybrid, Puffkin, Elderkin, Harefolk, Drakescale) at the opening choice. The starter asks to travel with the trainer, as the Hybrid arc already does in Saltquay.
2. Asking in towns and story beats: named Bondkin approach the trainer at guild halls, inns, markets, and after Trial Battles, and ask to join. The trainer says yes or no. Declining is free. The Bondkin may ask again later, except one-time story asks, which are gone once declined.
3. Dispatch returns: a dispatched Bondkin sometimes comes back with a new teammate who asks to join. See the dispatch recruitment section below.
4. Bazaar hiring: hire at the five hiring bazaars. See the bazaar section below.

Rules that follow from it:

- There are no wild Bondkin encounters. No Bondkin appears on a route, in a field, or in a dungeon to be found. Bondkin appear only in towns, guild halls, bazaars, dispatch returns, and story beats.
- There is no tracking, no searching, and no hunting mechanic for Bondkin. The Grimoire lists which bloodlines have asked to join and which have not, so the player can see what is missing without hunting.
- A caught Beast that is trained can ask to become a Bondkin partner. The Beast asks after training, by its own choice. Catching alone never makes a Beast a Bondkin. A Beast that never asks stays a Beast.
- A Bondkin that asks needs a free roster slot of 24. If the roster is full, the Bondkin waits where it asked (town, bazaar, or guild hall) until a slot opens.
- Guests are Mankind, not Bondkin, and are not covered by this rule. Pets never join as Bondkin.

## Dispatch recruitment: the 9 non-core bloodlines come home from missions

Git-FED's order, 2026-11-23: the non-playable races from the paste (Seeq, Gria, Aegyl, Garif, Rev/Revgaji, Baknamy, Urutan-Yensa, Helgas, Occuria) and the missing playable ones become creatures you get in the game, not by catching, but the FFTA way: when your team comes back from a dispatch mission, they sometimes come back with a new teammate. Those races become Bondkin you recruit through mission returns, not Beasts you catch.

FED-13 decision, building on the existing dispatch system in new-game.md (2 percent rare dispatch recruitment, cap 3, roster of 24):

- The rate and the cap stay: 2 percent per completed combat dispatch mission, flat, never rising with mission rank, Bond Lv, or the number of Bondkin sent, so it cannot be farmed. At most 3 recruits total from dispatch across the whole game. Once 3 have come in, the chance drops to 0 forever.
- The recruit pool is now defined: a dispatch recruit is always one of the 9 non-core bloodlines (Mudsnout, Wyrmwing, Skyborn, Furmask, Pardkin, Ashmask, Dunehusk, Fatewardens, Helgas). Core bloodlines never come from dispatch. You already get core bloodlines as starters, by asking in towns, and by hiring.
- No repeat bloodline from dispatch: the 3 recruits are 3 different bloodlines. If the roll picks a bloodline already recruited by dispatch, the roll picks again from the remaining pool.
- The recruit's bloodline comes from the mission world's pool. Each world has 1 to 2 recruit bloodlines tied to it:

| World | Dispatch recruit bloodlines |
| --- | --- |
| Realm Map | Mudsnout, Dunehusk |
| The Thirteen headquarters | Fatewardens, Helgas |
| The Thirteen's dark corridors | Ashmask, Helgas |
| The Thirteen's secrecy | Pardkin, Ashmask |
| Magic multiverse | Wyrmwing, Skyborn |
| Mind and emotions world | Fatewardens |
| Fairy tale kingdom | Wyrmwing |
| Hidden tech kingdom | Furmask, Wyrmwing |
| Lion kingdom savanna | Furmask, Dunehusk |
| Underwater kingdom | Mudsnout, Skyborn |
| Ocean island | Skyborn, Mudsnout |
| Animal city | Pardkin, Mudsnout |
| Haunted house | Fatewardens, Ashmask |

- Fatewardens and Helgas only come back from Rank 3 or higher missions, and only from the headquarters, the dark corridors, the mind and emotions world, and the haunted house. They are the rarest recruits.
- The recruit asks to join, following the ask-to-join rule. The returning Bondkin brings the recruit to the guild hall, the recruit asks the trainer, and the trainer says yes or no. Saying no does not use up one of the 3 recruit slots. The recruit leaves, and future missions can still bring one.
- The recruit is Lv 1 to 5, joins the clan roster of 24, and needs a free roster slot. If the roster is full, no recruit arrives, and the roll is not spent.
- Gathering missions never recruit. They collect materials only. Unchanged.
- Catching Beasts is still the real way to build a team. There is no cap on Beasts caught and no cap on Beasts that become Bondkin. Dispatch recruitment is a rare bonus, never a replacement for catching.

## Bazaars and hiring: five hiring bazaars, finite pool, player control

Git-FED's order, 2026-11-23, corrected the same day: there are MORE than five bazaars in the game total. Only five of them have people you can hire. The other bazaars are regular markets with no hiring. The finite hire pool and the "no more people to hire, you have hired everyone" message apply across those five hiring bazaars only. The point is to give the player more feeling of control than the original game had, where recruitment was random and out of the player's hands.

FED-13 decision:

- There are 13 bazaars in total, one per world, in each world's main town. 5 have hiring. 8 are regular markets with no hiring.
- The five hiring bazaars, and when they open:
  1. Saltquay Market, Realm Map. Opens after Trial Battle 1.
  2. Veilmarket, The Thirteen's secrecy. Opens when the player reaches the secrecy world.
  3. Pawford Market Square, Animal city. Opens when the player reaches the animal city.
  4. Coralport Tide Market, Underwater kingdom. Opens when the player reaches the underwater kingdom.
  5. Goldmane clan market, Lion kingdom savanna. Opens when the player reaches the savanna.
- The eight regular bazaars, no hiring, goods only: Vexhall (headquarters), Whitehollow (dark corridors), Portalwatch (magic multiverse), Stillmere (mind and emotions world), Thornwick (fairy tale kingdom), Gearford (hidden tech kingdom), Wavecrest (ocean island), Gravewick (haunted house).
- The hire pool is finite: 12 hireable Bondkin in total across the five hiring bazaars. Each one is a fixed named individual with a set bloodline, job, level, and fee. Nothing in the pool is random, and nothing in the pool ever respawns or restocks. Once all 12 are hired, that is it, no more.
- The 12 hires, one of each of the 9 non-core bloodlines plus 3 core bloodlines for early variety:
  - Saltquay Market (3): Mudsnout dock worker, Lv 3 Fighter, 500 Cr. Dunehusk oasis trader, Lv 4 Alchemist, 500 Cr. Drakescale guard, Lv 5 Warrior, 500 Cr.
  - Veilmarket (3): Pardkin courier, Lv 8 Thief, 1,500 Cr. Ashmask forger, Lv 9 Gadgeteer, 1,500 Cr. Fatewardens watcher, Lv 16 Sage, 3,000 Cr.
  - Pawford Market Square (2): Wyrmwing messenger, Lv 10 Dragoon, 1,500 Cr. Harefolk courier, Lv 7 Archer, 1,500 Cr.
  - Coralport Tide Market (2): Skyborn diver, Lv 11 White Mage, 1,500 Cr. Helgas warden, Lv 14 Paladin, 1,500 Cr.
  - Goldmane clan market (2): Furmask tracker, Lv 9 Hunter, 1,500 Cr. Elderkin sage, Lv 10 Black Mage, 1,500 Cr.
- Hiring fee by level tier: Lv 1 to 5 costs 500 Cr, Lv 6 to 15 costs 1,500 Cr, Lv 16 to 30 costs 3,000 Cr. Hiring costs Crests only, no quest and no item.
- The Hire board at a hiring bazaar lists that bazaar's unhired pool with name, bloodline, job, level, and fee. The player picks one, pays, and the Bondkin joins the roster of 24. A hired Bondkin needs a free roster slot. If the roster is full, the hire cannot be completed, and the Bondkin waits at the bazaar until a slot opens.
- Exhaustion message: when all 12 are hired, every hiring bazaar's Hire board shows the same line: "There are no more people to hire. You have hired everyone." The Hire option stays visible so the player can see the message, it is never removed.
- Hired Bondkin are ordinary clan members. They can change jobs, go on dispatch, and fight in team battles and turf battles like any other Bondkin. They cannot be sold at The Bank. Starters and story Bondkin are also never sellable, already decided.
- Hiring is the controlled route. Dispatch recruitment is the random route, capped at 3. Asking in towns is the story route. The three routes together cover all 9 non-core bloodlines: dispatch gives up to 3, bazaars give all 9, story asks give the rest.

## Turf: why it exists and why it stays fresh across 13 worlds

Git-FED's points, 2026-11-23. Two design points for the turf section of new-game.md.

### The point of having a turf

The whole point of having a turf is to unlock better missions, get cheaper items, and get access to closed-off places that were only meant for certain people, plus other perks. FED-13 decision, the full perk list:

- Better missions: owning 1 turf area in a world unlocks that world's Rank 3 dispatch and gathering missions. Owning all 3 areas in a world unlocks that world's Rank 4 missions and exclusive turf missions (defense and escort work) posted only in owned worlds.
- Cheaper items: shops in owned turf towns sell at a discount of 10 percent per owned area in that world, up to 30 percent off for a full world (3 of 3 areas).
- Access to restricted places: owning all 3 areas in a world opens that world's sealed place, a room or quarter closed to outsiders:
  - Realm Map: Saltquay Vault.
  - The Thirteen headquarters: Vexhall Sealed Archive.
  - The Thirteen's dark corridors: White Room Library.
  - The Thirteen's secrecy: Cipherest Ink Vault.
  - Magic multiverse: Portalwatch Gatehouse.
  - Mind and emotions world: Stillmere Mirror Shrine.
  - Fairy tale kingdom: Thornwick Rose Garden.
  - Hidden tech kingdom: Gearford Steam Vault.
  - Lion kingdom savanna: Goldmane Chief's Tent.
  - Underwater kingdom: Coralport Pearl Vault.
  - Ocean island: Wavecrest Star Chart Room.
  - Animal city: Pawford Detective Guild Back Room.
  - Haunted house: Gravewick Sealed Crypt.
- Other perks, decided by Muse: ask-to-join events happen twice as often in towns inside owned turf worlds, because Bondkin are more willing to approach a clan that holds the land. Owning 5 worlds grants the clan title Turf Warden. Owning all 13 worlds grants the clan title Worldkeeper.
- Existing perks stay: Crests payout (100 Cr per area per day, 350 Cr world bonus, 8,450 Cr per day for all 39 areas and 13 worlds), 1 turf item per area per day, training bonus (10 percent more experience on owned turf), starting plus 1 Path Point on owned turf, fast travel to owned turf, turf intel on rival clan movements, clan standing (1 per area, 5 per world).

### Inter-world turf defense keeps turf fresh

In FFTA you ran around the same map all day and it got old. In FED-13 the turf system is more interesting because there are different worlds, and you have to travel to different worlds to protect your turf. FED-13 decision, stated in the turf section:

- Rival clan challenges rotate world by world, so defenses are spread across the map instead of stacking in one world. A challenge cycle never hits the same world twice in a row.
- A defense notice gives until the end of the next day, so defending a far world means traveling there: Sky Galleon between worlds at 800 Cr per voyage, Sky Skiff inside a world. The travel cost and the time are the intended friction, not an accident.
- Because turf spans 39 areas in 13 worlds, the player is always moving: one day a defense in the savanna, the next a challenge in the underwater kingdom, the next a banner hold in the haunted house. The variety of worlds is what keeps turf fresh, unlike FFTA's single map.
