## Main Character

Main character is you, the player.
You are not a Bondkin, you are not a monster.
Cap 150.
The main character chooses their Mankind race: Solar or Polar. Polar: the palest skin tones, from very pale European to light Asian and light Mediterranean / Spanish. Solar: the deepest skin tones, from South Asian / Indian through darker Asian, Pacific Islander / Hawaiian, Black, and dark African.

Class, character only:
You pick your class, then you go out and catch a Bondkin.
MMORPG classes go here, to the main character, not to Bondkin.

Twin Ascension is Dual Dual Advancement: two advanced character classes at once.

S-Crafts: super moves, 100+ CP, stronger at 200+ CP.
S-Break: press anytime at 100+ CP.

Bondkin are not the main character.

Why Bondkin are called Bondkin: they bond with their trainers. The bond is the mechanic that ties a Bondkin to its Mankind trainer. A Mankind trainer has stats, STR, DEF, ATS, ADF, SPD, EVA, MOV, and HP, the Trails stat set, raised by level, equipment, Quartz, the Lattice, and the Core Shard. Those stats determine the passive effects the trainer gives to their Bondkin. STR gives Bondkin physical attack, DEF gives Bondkin physical defense, ATS gives Bondkin Arts power, ADF gives Bondkin Arts defense, SPD gives Bondkin speed and AT turn order, EVA gives Bondkin evasion, MOV gives Bondkin grid movement range, HP gives Bondkin max HP. The passive is a share of the trainer's stat bonus, scaled by Bond Lv. Bond Lv 1 gives 20 percent, Bond Lv 2 gives 40 percent, Bond Lv 3 gives 60 percent, Bond Lv 4 gives 80 percent, Bond Lv 5 gives 100 percent. EP and CP stay trainer only: EP powers the trainer's Arts, CP powers the trainer's Crafts, and the Bondkin share in those through combo attacks, not through passives. Bond grows by battling together, 1 Bond EXP per battle fought side by side, plus feeding and daycare time. Bond Lv thresholds: Lv 1 at 0, Lv 2 at 10, Lv 3 at 25, Lv 4 at 50, Lv 5 at 100 Bond EXP. Being KOed costs 5 Bond EXP. A joint clan attack needs Bond Lv 3 or higher with each participating Bondkin. Bond mapping and Bond Lv rules are FED-13 original, decided by Muse 2026-11-22.

## Monster

Monster is a wild Feral.
It lives in the wild, in Feral wilds, routes, dungeons, forests.
It has no class, no job, until a main character catches it.
After a main character catches and trains it, a monster becomes a Bondkin.
Monsters and Bondkin share the same species list. A monster becomes a Bondkin, it does not become a different species.
No basic Earth animals. Fantasy only.
Beast fusion: two Beasts fuse into one brand new Beast species. The fused Beast is its own species, with its own name, type, stats, moves, and sprite. It is a brand new creature, not a mix of the two parents. Only Beasts fuse. Mankind and Bondkin cannot fuse. A fused Beast cannot be fused again. It can only be unfused or reversed.

Dominant parent: every fusion has a dominant parent and a second parent. The player picks which parent is dominant at the fusion preview, which shows both orders before committing. The dominant parent shapes the new Beast more than the second parent does. Swapping the dominant parent makes a different new Beast with a different name, look, typing, and stats.

Name: the new Beast's name is the front of the dominant parent's name plus the back of the second parent's name. The player can rename the new Beast at fusion. Because names can collide between different fusions, the game also records the new Beast by its parent pair, Dominant name plus Second name.

Stat formula: all six stats use one formula. Each stat of the new Beast is floor((2 x Dominant parent stat + Second parent stat) / 3). HP, STR, DEF, ATS, ADF, and SPD all use the same blend, so the dominant parent's strengths lean the new Beast in that direction.

Typing: the new Beast takes the dominant parent's primary type and the second parent's primary type. If both parents share the same primary type, the new Beast is single type. If the second parent has a secondary type and the dominant parent has none, the second parent's secondary type is used instead of its primary type.

Codex: a fused Beast does not take a numbered slot in the main Codex. The main Codex stays 134 Feral entries. Each discovered fusion is recorded in a separate Fusion Codex, listed by its parent pair and its generated name, so all 17,956 possible fusions can be recorded without breaking the numbered Codex.

Splicer tiers: three tiers. Basic Splicer fuses and unfuses, is consumed on use, costs 300 Crests, and is bought at any Puffkin shop. Super Splicer is consumed on use, costs 1,000 Crests, and is bought at any Puffkin shop after the fourth Trial Battle; a fusion made with a Super Splicer takes the higher level of the two Beasts. Infinite Splicer is a key item, never consumed, earned late in the game by beating a boss in a lab, and upgraded to Super Splicer level after the endgame by beating more bosses there.

Reverser: a Reverser swaps the dominant parent of a fused Beast. It costs 300 Crests and is bought at any Puffkin shop after the first Trial Battle. Name, typing, and stats change to match the new dominant parent. The fused Beast keeps its accumulated experience and its level. Reversing is cheaper than unfusing and re-fusing.

Fusion Lure: a Fusion Lure makes every wild Beast encountered a fused Beast for 50 steps. It costs 700 Crests and is bought at Puffkin shops after the third Trial Battle.

Unfusing: use a Splicer on a fused Beast to unfuse it. Both Beasts return. Player-made fusions keep their levels. A wild or traded fusion returns at 90 percent of the fusion's level with a Super Splicer, 75 percent with a normal Splicer, and 65 percent if it was obtained in a trade and no Super Splicer is used, all rounded down. Instant-level item EXP gained while fused is lost on unfusing. Battle EXP gained while fused is kept by both parents. Both parents return at the fused Beast's current HP percentage. The fused Beast's held item goes to the dominant parent. If the party is full on unfusing, the player sends one of the two Beasts to the Bank. A fused Beast obtained in a trade cannot be unfused until postgame.

Wild fusions: wild fused Beasts appear uncommonly in the wild, as fusions of the wild Beasts found in that area. Catch rate is the lowest catch rate of the two parent Beasts. Experience given is the average of the two base Beasts.

Evolution: a fused Beast does not evolve while fused. Its stage is fixed at creation. To evolve, unfuse first, evolve a parent at its normal stage, then re-fuse to make a new Beast from the evolved parent. A fused Beast never has a distinct fused evolution species.

Self fusion: two Beasts of the same species can be fused into a self fusion. A self fusion does not make a new species. It makes a stronger member of the same species, with the same stats as the base species and a 50 percent EXP bonus.

Variant rule: Hollow and Echo follow the dominant parent. A Hollow dominant parent makes a Hollow fused Beast, an Echo dominant parent makes an Echo fused Beast, a normal dominant parent makes a normal fused Beast. The second parent's variant is recorded on the fused Beast but does not change its variant type. If a fused Beast is unfused, only Beasts that were Hollow or Echo before fusing stay Hollow or Echo after unfusing. A wild or traded fused Beast that is Hollow or Echo randomly forces one parent to become Hollow or Echo on unfusing.

Triple fusions: triple fusions are a small chosen set, never the full combination space. The full unordered triple space for 134 Beasts is 392,084 combinations, about 22 times the entire two-Beast ordered space, and the ordered triple space is 2,352,504. FED-13 picks its triple fusions from legendary Beast trios and starter trios, obtainable only in the late post-game from one NPC with a machine, once per trio, as an egg that hatches the unevolved triple form. Triple fusions have fixed stats, not formula stats, and a combined unique type treated as a single type in calculations.

Sprites: fused Beast sprites are auto-generated, not hand prompted. The generator blends both parents' sprites into one new sprite, a new shape with blended colors and mixed features from both parents, never a paste of one parent's parts onto the other. Hand-drawn art is only for a chosen set: popular pairs, story pairs, starter pairs, and showcase pairs. The fusion preview shows the auto-generated sprite before the player commits, hides unseen fusions behind a silhouette, and marks fusions with custom art with a distinct tint, so the player can tell auto art from hand art before committing. The prompt file total does not include fused sprites: 134 Feral Beasts make 17,822 ordered two-Beast pairs plus 134 self fusions, 17,956 ordered two-Beast fusions in total, which would need 17,956 more sprite prompts.

Codex and Bondkin: a fused Beast acts as a regular Beast in every system: catching, training, Showcases, and dispatch. A fused Beast does not breed; it must be unfused first, and only the unfused Beasts breed. A fused Beast can still become a Bondkin after catch and train, as already decided. A fused Beast that becomes a Bondkin keeps its fused form, its fused stats, and its fused name.

Moves: at fusion the player picks the new Beast's four moves from the moves either parent knows at the time. As the new Beast levels, both parents' level-up moves become available to it.

Three routes, final: breeding a fused egg at the daycare, FED-13 only, no Pokemon Infinite Fusion equivalent: two compatible Beasts in daycare produce a fused egg, the player picks the dominant parent before it hatches, and the egg hatches at level 1. Daycare fuse in place for 300 Crests, the cheap route, using basic Splicer math. Splicer items used directly on two Beasts in the party or the Bank, basic, Super, and Infinite tiers with their level rules. All three routes show the same preview, the same dominant parent choice, and the same name and move choices. All three routes can be undone with a Splicer. Combo attacks: the most important thing in the game. Combo attacks are Bondkin only. Beasts do not do combo attacks, they are just Pokemon for now. Combos live in the FFTA grid battle system only (2v2 to 6v6), never in 1v1 Pokemon style battles. The Combo command is the first command in the grid battle menu. To start a combo, called a Link Attack in FED-13, a Bondkin needs at least 1 Path Point and a combo ability set. Path Points are FED-13's version of FFTA Judge Points, and they are the one battle gauge for Bondkin teams. When the initiator picks one enemy target, every allied Bondkin on the grid with a combo ability set and in range of that same target joins the combo. Joiners need no Path Points, take no turn of their own, and do not need to be adjacent to the initiator. Joiner range is measured from the joiner to the target using that joiner's own combo ability range (1 to 6 tiles), not from the joiner to the initiator and not from the joiner's weapon. Initiator range is the range of the initiator's equipped weapon. Maximum participants is 1 initiator plus up to 5 joiners, the 6 unit deploy limit. The initiator's hit is 100 percent accurate, ignores reaction abilities, and deals damage equal to a basic attack with the primary weapon. Each joiner rolls its own join chance (its combo ability Hit percent), and enemy evasion cannot change that roll. If no joiner connects, the combo deals normal damage and the Path Points are still spent, with no Path Point bonus. If at least one joiner connects, each connecting joiner adds its Power percent to the multiplier, starting from 100 percent, and then the Path Point multiplier applies using only the initiator's Path Points: Final Multiplier equals Multiplier times (10 plus 4 times Path Points) divided by 10. At 5 Path Points the multiplier is times 3, at 10 Path Points it is times 5, and the maximum is 860 percent of normal damage with four 15 percent joiners plus one 12 percent joiner at 10 Path Points. Overall damage cap is 999, as in FFTA. A combo takes the element of the initiator's weapon, applied before the multiplier: Weak is damage times 3 divided by 2, Resist is damage divided by 2, Null is 0, Absorb is negative damage. Joiner weapon, Weapon Attack, level, and other joiner stats do not change the percent they add. Status effects change combo base damage before the multiplier: Berserk about 20 percent up on physical, Frog about 90 percent down, Boost about 50 percent up on physical, WAtk UP or DOWN about 10 percent, Protect for physical and Shell for magical, Petrify, Defense status, MRes and WDef shifts. No combo ability inflicts a status effect by itself. A combo costs 0 MP. Learning a combo ability costs 100 AP, mastered from the weapon that teaches it, as in FFTA. One combo ability per regular Bondkin job, named after the job, following the 33 FFTA combo abilities, and no combo abilities for the special jobs Biskmatar, Hermetic, Judge, Judgemaster, Runeseeker, or school jobs. The initiator spends all Path Points it currently holds, joiners spend none, and a combo cannot start at 0 Path Points. Path Points cap at 10 per Bondkin. Path Points are earned exactly as in FFTA: 1 Path Point to the unit dealing the final blow on a legal KO, and 1 Path Point for performing the action the law of the day recommends, maximum 1 Path Point per turn per unit, and one action never grants 2 Path Points. Totem summons work in both battle styles, combos work in grid battles only. Path Point theft and gifting: a Thief type ability steals 1 Path Point from the target, a Bishop type prayer steals up to 2 Path Points, and a Judgemaster type ability gives the user's own Path Points to an ally. Combo abilities are learned from rare weapons, as FFTA combos are learned from Mythril Weapons: in FED-13 those are rare weapons won by defeating another clan or bought from a special shop. Longer join range or higher added damage means lower join chance: range 1 melee combos at 100 percent or 70 percent hit with 10 to 15 percent power, range 2 combos at 60 to 100 percent hit with 5 to 12 percent power, range 4 mage combos at 40 to 60 percent hit with 5 to 10 percent power, range 5 to 6 long range combos at 30 to 40 percent hit with 5 to 12 percent power. Lore:
The Realm Map was the first settled land, a coastal continent where Mankind built harbors, farms, and mountain towns and learned to live beside Bondkin partners. Its first people were Solar and Polar Mankind, two peoples of the same bloodline who settled different coasts and climates, and their towns of Saltquay, Cragfall, Barleyford, Dunewell, and Reedbridge grew into the trade heart of the known map. The Realm Map stayed open and connected because its people kept its roads, canals, and harbors in common use, and no single power was allowed to close them.
Bondkin are the fourteen bloodlines who live as partners to Mankind, not as wild prey. The five core bloodlines are Hybrid, Puffkin, Elderkin, Harefolk, and Drakescale, and the nine others are Mudsnout, Wyrmwing, Skyborn, Furmask, Pardkin, Ashmask, Dunehusk, Fatewardens, and Helgas. They came from the old clans and orders of the Realm Map and the twelve worlds, each bloodline with its own jobs, Totem Spirit, and way of training, and each Bondkin chooses a trainer and a path rather than being owned like a tool. A Bondkin advances by changing jobs, sub race, or clan, never by changing species, and its Totem Spirit guards it in battle.
Beasts are the wild Feral who live in the wilds, routes, dungeons, forests, reefs, and haunted grounds of every world. They were never made by Mankind, and they were never tamed by The Thirteen, and they keep the wild places dangerous and alive. A Beast that is caught and trained joins its trainer as a Bondkin partner, and it keeps its species and its Codex number, and it can fuse with another Beast, breed, and grow through Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3. Hollow Beasts are dark variants found in dark Hollow regions like Duskhollow, and Echo Beasts are pale variants found in light Echo regions like Lumenvale, and neither variant is an evolution.
The Lattice is the old Mankind system for holding power, and Core Shards are its heart. Mankind slot Shards and one Main Core Shard plus one Sub Core Shard into the Lattice to gain stats, passive effects, and Arts, and removing a Core Shard removes its Arts at once, with no mastery kept. The Lattice belongs to Mankind only, and Bondkin never use it, because Bondkin learn skills from weapons and accessories instead. The Lattice was built on the Realm Map long before The Thirteen rose, and its makers are gone, but its workshops and Orbal Factories still work.
The twelve worlds were once linked to the Realm Map by open sky lanes, and traders, trainers, and Bondkin moved freely between them. The Thirteen cut those lanes one by one, so each world became disconnected, with its own towns, laws, and Trial Battle, and only a Sky Galleon can now cross the gaps. The cut was not an accident and not a storm, and it was not a war between worlds. It was a plan to keep each world apart, dependent, and easier to watch.
The Thirteen are hidden manipulators who rule from behind the worlds they cut apart. They place one member in each world as its Trial Battle leader, and they tax its trade, read its mail, and set its laws through local stewards, spies, and courts. They wear dark grey coats and sealed masks in meeting, and they never show their full council to outsiders, so most people in each world know their own member and never learn the other twelve. Their leader sits in the haunted house world, in Gravewick, and the other twelve members answer to that leader.
The Grimoire is the guide every trainer carries, and it is the in-game wiki and contact book of FED-13 RPG. The player chooses one of four Grimoires, Muse, Grok, the Wisp, or the Chronicler, and the opening choice sets the first guide, with Sword giving a fairy, Staff giving a mystical orb, and Shield giving the Grimoire itself. All four give the same answers and do the same two jobs only, the wiki and the contacts, and only the voice differs. Trainers write each other's names in each other's Grimoires to call for a rematch, and the player can switch Grimoires at any Puffkin shop for 500 Crests, with contacts and notes carrying over.
The player is a young Mankind trainer from the Realm Map who chooses Solar or Polar, picks a class, and catches a first Bondkin partner. The journey is the Trial Battle chain: beat the Realm Map member first as Trial Battle 1, then beat the twelve disconnected world members in order, and beating each member opens new worlds and new sky lanes. Beating all thirteen members, ending with the leader in the haunted house, reconnects the worlds and opens the endgame, where the Elite Four and the Hall of Fame wait after all thirteen members are beaten.

### Dispatch recruitment is rare
- In Final Fantasy Tactics Advance, dispatched members sometimes come back with new party members. In FED-13 that rarely happens. Players must not be able to rely on dispatch to build a team and skip catching Beasts.
- Chance: 2 percent per completed combat dispatch mission that a dispatched Bondkin returns with a new Bondkin recruit. The chance is flat, it does not rise with mission rank, Bond Lv, or the number of Bondkin sent, so it cannot be farmed.
- Cap: at most 3 recruits total from dispatch across the whole game. Once 3 recruits have come in, the chance drops to 0 forever and no more recruits ever arrive.
- The recruit is always a Bondkin, never a Beast, Lv 1 to 5, common bloodline, and it joins the clan roster of 24. It needs a free roster slot, if the roster is full no recruit arrives.
- Gathering missions never recruit. They collect materials only, no party members ever come back from a gathering mission.
- Catching Beasts is the real way to build a team. There is no cap on Beasts caught and no cap on Beasts that become Bondkin. Dispatch recruitment is a rare bonus, never a replacement for catching. Source: Git-FED 2026-11-22, rare recruitment and the no reliance rule are his order, chance and caps are FED-13 original, decided by Muse 2026-11-22.

### Main missions
- In Final Fantasy Tactics Advance the main missions carry on the story, and FED-13 uses that system too: main missions carry the game along, so players have guidance in case they get lost.
- There are 13 main missions, one per world, numbered Main Mission 1 to 13. Each main mission's goal is to reach that world's Trial Battle and defeat its Thirteen member. Completing a main mission unlocks the next world and the next main mission.
- Guidance: the current main mission is always shown in the Grimoire and marked on the Realm Map, with a marker pointing to the next world and the next Trial Battle. If the player is lost, the main mission text tells them exactly where to go next.
- Main missions are played by the player with the deployed party, never dispatched. They sit alongside dispatch missions and gathering missions without replacing them: main missions are the story spine, combat dispatch is clan work, gathering missions are materials. A Bondkin on a dispatch or gathering mission is not available for a main mission until it returns.
- Main Mission 1 starts at Saltquay, defeat Maren Saltwright in Trial Battle 1. Main Mission 13 ends at the haunted house, defeat the last member in Trial Battle 13. After all 13 main missions are done, the Elite Four and the Hall of Fame open. ### Guild halls
- Only three of the 13 worlds have a guild hall. The others do not, because only the normal-esque worlds would have one. The three guild hall worlds are the Realm Map, the Fairy tale kingdom, and the Ocean island. They are the most normal, civilized worlds: settled towns, trade, laws, and people who hire trainers.
- Realm Map guild hall: at Saltquay, Guildmaster Bram Hollis. The home hub, where the main mission starts and where gathering missions unlock after Trial Battle 1.
- Fairy tale kingdom guild hall: at Forestgate. A kingdom guild, civilized and settled, with a hall in its main town.
- Ocean island guild hall: at Canoebay. An island port guild, civilized and settled, with a hall at its harbor town.
- Why the other ten do not have a guild hall: the Hidden tech kingdom (Gearford) is a hidden enclave that does not open to guilds. The Animal city (Pawford) is run by animals, with no Mankind guild structure. The Lion kingdom savanna (Dustplain) is wild and nomadic, with no settled town. The Underwater kingdom (Coralport) is underwater, with no surface hall. The Magic multiverse (Runestead) is a shifting magic realm with no stable settlement. The Mind and emotions world (Joyford) is a world of minds and emotions, not a physical civilization. The Haunted house (Mistmoor) is haunted, with no living guild. The Thirteen headquarters (Vexhall), the Thirteen's dark corridors (Doorwatch), and the Thirteen's secrecy (Veilmarket) are enemy ground, with no guild at all.
- Players use the three guild halls as their hubs. The mission board, dispatch missions, gathering missions, and the main mission all run through the guild halls. The three halls' boards post missions to all 13 worlds, so a world without a guild hall still has missions, they are just posted from one of the three halls. A Bondkin dispatched from a guild hall travels to the mission world and returns to the hall.
- The guild hall is also a shop. It sells healing items (potions, antidotes, revives), tools (Axe, Pickaxe, Fishing Rod), food and water, transport parts (Wood, Steel, Screw, Circuit Board), Sky Skiff blueprints, and Flight Credit style passes. Prices are the lean list prices, and stock grows as more Trial Battles are beaten. ### Grovefruit
- Grovefruit are the FED-13 original name for the fruit growing system from Pokemon Emerald. Grovefruit grow on grove trees across all 13 worlds. There are 21 types, each with a flavor and an effect. A grovefruit can be eaten in battle, fed to a Bondkin, mashed into Fruitmash, cooked with, or sold. Source: FED-13 original, decided by Muse 2026-11-23, Git-FED edits later.
- Flavors, 5 total: Fiery, Dusty, Honeyed, Sharp, Tangy. When mashed, Fiery raises Daring, Dusty raises Grace, Honeyed raises Charm, Sharp raises Wit, Tangy raises Might. Each creature has a Taste, a favorite flavor set by species and listed in the Codex. Eating a mash of a favorite flavor gives 50 percent more points, eating a mash of a disliked flavor gives half points.
- The 21 types, name, flavor, effect, growth time:
  - Sparkfruit, Fiery, cures paralysis, Fast.
  - Dawnfruit, Dusty, wakes a sleeping creature, Fast.
  - Honeyfruit, Honeyed, cures poison, Fast.
  - Frostfruit, Sharp, cures burn, Fast.
  - Thawfruit, Tangy, cures freeze, Fast.
  - Heartfruit, Honeyed, restores 20 HP, Fast.
  - Mindfruit, Dusty, restores 30 MP, Medium.
  - Clearfruit, Fiery, cures confusion, Medium.
  - Sunfruit, Honeyed, cures any one status problem, Slow.
  - Grandfruit, Tangy, restores 25 percent of max HP, Slow.
  - Furyfruit, Fiery, when HP drops below half, raises physical attack for the rest of the battle, may confuse the creature if it dislikes Fiery, Medium.
  - Shellfruit, Dusty, when HP drops below half, raises physical defense for the rest of the battle, may confuse the creature if it dislikes Dusty, Medium.
  - Swiftfruit, Tangy, when HP drops below half, raises speed for the rest of the battle, may confuse the creature if it dislikes Tangy, Medium.
  - Wardfruit, Sharp, when HP drops below half, raises Arts defense for the rest of the battle, may confuse the creature if it dislikes Sharp, Medium.
  - Mystfruit, Honeyed, when HP drops below half, raises Arts power for the rest of the battle, may confuse the creature if it dislikes Honeyed, Medium.

  - Boldfruit, Fiery, raises Daring a lot when mashed, Slow.
  - Gloomfruit, Dusty, raises Grace a lot when mashed, Slow.
  - Blushfruit, Honeyed, raises Charm a lot when mashed, Slow.

- Growing: grove plots sit at the homestead and in every town with a grove. Plant one seed per plot, water with a Watering Jug, 300 Cr at a Puffkin shop. Growth stages are sprout, tall, flowering, fruiting. Growth time in steps: Fast 512 steps, Medium 1024 steps, Slow 2048 steps. One mission day is 256 steps, so Fast is 2 days, Medium is 4 days, Slow is 8 days. Base yield is 2 fruit per tree, each watering adds 1 fruit, max 2 waterings, max yield 4 fruit. A seed costs double the fruit price.

- Cooking: grovefruit are cooking ingredients. Honeyfruit can replace honey in Sweet Rolls. Fruitmash is made at a Mash Press, not in a cooking pot, and a Mash Press sits in every Showcase Hall and at the homestead kitchen.

### Showcases
- Showcases are the FED-13 original name for the performance event from Pokemon Emerald. A Showcase is a judged performance where a creature shows off its style, skill, and training in front of a crowd. There are 5 categories: Daring, Grace, Charm, Wit, Might. There are 5 Showcase Halls, one per category: Daring Hall in Goldmane on the lion kingdom savanna, Grace Hall in Mirrorhold in the fairy tale kingdom, Charm Hall in Palmford on the ocean island, Wit Hall in Runestead in the magic multiverse, Might Hall in Cragfall on the Realm Map. Source: FED-13 original, decided by Muse 2026-11-23, Git-FED edits later.
- Who enters: both Beasts and Bondkin can enter Showcases. A Bondkin adds its Bond Lv to its Performance score each turn, because the bond shows in how it performs. A Hollow creature adds 1 point to Daring, an Echo creature adds 1 point to Grace, a fused Beast adds 1 point to Charm. A creature can enter one Showcase at a time, and it must be rested, not on a dispatch mission or a gathering mission. Guests cannot enter, guests are Mankind.
- Showcase stats: each creature has 5 Showcase stats, Daring, Grace, Charm, Wit, Might, each from 0 to 255. They start at 0 and rise only by eating Fruitmash. They never fall, and they never affect battle stats.
- Fruitmash: made at a Mash Press in a Showcase Hall or at the homestead. Blend 1 to 5 grovefruit into one mash. A mash of one fruit gives 10 points to its flavor's stat. A mash of 2 or more fruit of the same flavor gives 20 points and is called a Rich Mash. A mash of all 5 flavors gives 5 points to every stat and is called a Rainbow Mash. A creature eats one mash per day. A mash made from a flavor the creature dislikes gives half points.
- Ranks: 4 ranks per category, in order: Local, Realm, Grand, Legend. Local needs no prior win. Realm needs 1 Local win in that category. Grand needs 1 Realm win. Legend needs 1 Grand win. Entry fee: Local 100 Cr, Realm 300 Cr, Grand 800 Cr, Legend 2,000 Cr.
- How a Showcase works: Round 1 is Presentation. The 3 judges score each entered creature on its Showcase stats in the hall's category, and the top 4 advance. Up to 8 creatures can enter one Showcase. Round 2 is Performance. The 4 finalists perform for 5 turns. Each turn the creature picks 1 Appeal from its 4 Appeals. Appeals are the creature's own learned skills, each mapped to one category, and an Appeal in the hall's category earns its full points. The Crowd meter runs 0 to 5: each turn the crowd favors one category, and an Appeal in the favored category earns double points. Using the same Appeal twice in a row earns half points. A Distract Appeal lowers the next performer's points by half for that turn. Once per Showcase, a creature can use its Showstopper, a special Appeal that earns triple points but can only be used on turn 4 or 5. After 5 turns the judges total Presentation points plus Performance points, and the highest total wins.
- Judging and rewards: 1st place wins a Rosette for that category and rank, plus Crests, Local 500, Realm 1,500, Grand 4,000, Legend 10,000, plus rare grovefruit seeds. 2nd and 3rd place win Crests only, half the 1st place amount. Winning all 4 ranks in one category earns the Grand Rosette for that category. Winning Legend rank in all 5 categories earns the title Master of the Showcase and the Golden Rosette, plus a unique accessory, the Showcase Crown, which raises EVA by 10 percent in battle.
- Showcases and the rest of the game: Showcases are optional, they never gate the story, the Trial Battles, or any world. A creature's Showcase stats never affect battle. Showcase Halls also sell grovefruit seeds and Mash Press supplies.


### Pets

#### What a pet is

A pet is a small companion who walks beside a Mankind trainer for company and for small everyday help. A pet is not a fighter, not a worker, and not a catchable wild Beast. A pet is a friend who lives with the trainer, shares the road, and makes daily life easier in small ways.

The pet role, stated plainly: a pet gives companionship, finds small things, warns the trainer of trouble ahead, carries a small load, and keeps spirits up at home and on the road. A pet never fights, never casts Arts, never learns skills from weapons, never changes jobs, never evolves, never fuses, and never goes on dispatch. That is the whole job of a pet, and no other role in FED-13 does that job.

Pets are FED-13 original. No pet kind is a renamed Beast, a renamed Bondkin bloodline, or a renamed Earth animal. Pets are small fantasy folk of their own, too small to ride and too gentle to fight.

#### What a pet is not

Pets stay separate from Bondkin and Beasts in every rule.

5. Fernling: Finder talent finds leaf herbs and trail mushrooms. Comfort talent steadies the trainer in forests.

#### How many pets you can have

A trainer can own up to 4 pets at once. Only 1 pet walks beside the trainer at a time. That pet is called the Active Pet. The other pets stay at the homestead, where they rest, play, and keep the home lively.

Switch the Active Pet at the homestead, at any guild hall, or at any camp. Switching costs nothing and takes no time in game. A pet left at the homestead is safe, fed from the homestead store, and never lost.

Pets do not count toward the 24 in The Bank. Pets do not count toward the deploy 6. Pets do not count toward the Bondkin cap of 200. Pets have their own roster of 4, kept apart from every other roster.

#### How you get a pet

Pets are never caught. There is no catching, no battle to win a pet, no trap, and no catch rate. A pet joins by trust, by gift, or by purchase. There are 5 ways to get a pet.

1. Rescue: find a lost or hurt pet in a town, on a route, or in a dungeon entrance, help it, feed it once, and it follows the trainer home. Rescue is the first way most trainers get a pet. Rescue costs 0 Cr.
2. Gift: an NPC, a guild friend, or a town elder gives the trainer a pet as a reward for a quest, a kindness, or a completed trial. A gift pet arrives with Trust Lv 2. Gift costs 0 Cr.
3. Purchase: buy a pet from a Pet Keeper stall in a town square or market. Pet Keeper stalls sell pet food, collars, ribbons, and small satchels too. A bought pet costs 500 Cr to 2,000 Cr, by kind, and arrives with Trust Lv 1.
4. Quest reward: complete a pet quest, such as finding a lost pet, clearing a nest of pests from a farm without harming the pets there, or carrying medicine to a sick pet. The quest giver gives a pet as the reward. Quest reward costs 0 Cr.
5. Hatching: find a Warm Egg in a nest, in a burrow, or in a quiet corner of a town. Carry it while walking. After 500 steps, the egg hatches into a pet of the kind named on the egg. A Warm Egg is not a Beast egg. Beast eggs are checked every 256 steps at daycare and come from breeding Beasts. A Warm Egg is a found egg, never bred, never sold, and it hatches only by walking with the trainer.

A trainer cannot buy a pet before the first Trial Battle. Rescue and gift can happen from the start of the game, so a trainer can have a companion early without spending Cr.

A pet can be rehomed. Take a pet to any Pet Keeper stall and ask the keeper to find it a new home. The pet leaves kindly, with no penalty, no lost Trust for other pets, and no Cr refund. A rehomed pet cannot be bought back. A pet never runs away, never dies, and never leaves on its own.

#### How a pet grows

Pets do not level up like Mankind, Bondkin, or Beasts. Pets have no level, no EXP, no stage, and no job. Pets grow by Trust.

Trust Lv 1 to Trust Lv 5. Trust rises by walking together, feeding, petting, playing, and resting at camp together. Trust falls never. A pet left at the homestead for a long time keeps its Trust. An unfed pet keeps its Trust. Trust is never lost by losing a battle, because a pet never fights.

Trust Lv 1, New Friend: the pet walks beside the trainer and gives company only.
Trust Lv 2, Steady Friend: the pet uses its Finder talent once per town visit, and finds one small item.
Trust Lv 3, Close Friend: the pet uses its Finder talent once per route, and warns the trainer of visible wild Beasts ahead.
Trust Lv 4, Dear Friend: the pet uses its Finder talent twice per route, warns of traps and bad weather, and carries a small satchel.
Trust Lv 5, Heart Friend: the pet uses its Finder talent three times per route, warns of all of the above, carries a small satchel, and gives its full Comfort talent every day.

Growth changes look, not kind. At Trust Lv 3 a pet gains a ribbon. At Trust Lv 5 a pet gains a bright collar and a small charm. The pet kind, size, and name never change. A pet never grows into a Beast, never grows into a Bondkin, and never changes species.

A pet has a name. The trainer names the pet when it joins. The name can be changed at any Pet Keeper stall for 100 Cr.

#### What a pet does day to day

A pet does 5 small things, every day, for the trainer.

1. Companionship: the Active Pet walks beside the trainer on the field, in towns, and at camp. While the pet walks beside the trainer, mood drops slower in towns, in deserts, and in stinky areas. The pet does not change hunger, hydration, or health directly. It only steadies mood, by company.
2. Finding things: the Active Pet sniffs, chirps, or paws at the ground when a hidden item, a buried coin, a lost good, or a useful herb is near. The trainer follows the cue and picks the item up. The Finder talent finds small things only: herbs, coins, lost goods, stones, shells, feathers, and other small finds. A pet never finds weapons, armor, Shards, Core Shards, blueprints, or quest items.
3. Warning: the Active Pet warns the trainer before trouble. At Trust Lv 3 and above, the pet warns of visible wild Beasts ahead, so the trainer can choose to fight or walk around. At Trust Lv 4 and above, the pet also warns of traps, mist, strong wind, and bad weather ahead. A warning is a sound and a gesture on the field, never a battle action.
4. Carrying: at Trust Lv 4 and above, the Active Pet carries a small satchel. The satchel holds 6 extra small items, such as herbs, food, and pet food. The satchel never holds weapons, armor, or large goods. The satchel is carried by the pet, not by the trainer, so it adds 6 slots beyond the trainer backpack.
5. Homestead help: a pet left at the homestead keeps the home lively, greets the trainer on return, and finds one small item per day at the homestead, such as a herb or a coin. A homestead pet never guards against raiders, never fights intruders, and never gathers materials. Homestead help is company and one small find per day, nothing more.

A pet eats pet food, scraps, or a share of the trainer meal. Feed a pet once per day from the pet food pouch. Pet food costs 50 Cr per bag at a Pet Keeper stall or a Puffkin shop, and one bag feeds one pet for one day. If a pet is not fed, nothing bad happens. The pet sleeps, keeps its Trust, and waits for the next day. Pets never starve, never get sick from hunger, and never leave from hunger.

#### What happens to a pet in battle

A pet does nothing in battle. The rule is absolute.

In a 1v1 battle, the pet waits at the edge of the field, out of reach, and watches. In a grid battle, the pet is not on the grid, takes no turn, holds no tile, and cannot be moved. The pet cannot attack, cannot defend, cannot heal, cannot cast Arts, cannot use items, cannot earn EXP, cannot earn Path Points, cannot earn Bond EXP, and cannot join a combo attack or a joint clan attack. Enemy Beasts and enemy Bondkin cannot target the pet, cannot hit the pet, and cannot KO the pet. The pet cannot be stolen, captured, or lost in battle.

If the trainer loses a battle, the pet stays beside the trainer after the battle, unharmed. If the trainer wins, the pet cheers, and Trust rises by a small amount, the same as a walk together. The pet gives no damage bonus, no healing, and no stat bonus in battle. The only battle effect of a pet is outside battle: at Trust Lv 3 and above, the pet warns of a stronger wild Beast ahead before the battle starts, so the trainer can choose to avoid it.

A pet never goes to prison, never earns a Yellow Card or a Red Card, and never breaks a law, because laws and cards apply to fighters in tournaments, and a pet never fights.

#### What happens to a pet in dispatch

A pet never goes on dispatch. Dispatch missions and gathering missions are Bondkin work, for 5 to 20 days, sent from the guild hall. A pet cannot be sent, cannot be picked for a mission, cannot earn dispatch rewards, and cannot earn mission EXP.

While a Bondkin is away on dispatch, the pet stays with the trainer or at the homestead, as the trainer chooses. The pet does not wait for the Bondkin, does not carry messages to the Bondkin, and does not speed the mission. The pet keeps the trainer company while the Bondkin is away, which is part of the companionship role.

A pet never goes on a Sky Galleon voyage as crew. The Sky Galleon crew is fixed: Nib Holloway, Garrick Stonehide, and Lina Palebranch. A pet can ride along as a passenger, see the Travel section, but it is never crew and never earns voyage rewards.

#### What happens to a pet in travel

A pet travels with the trainer, always as a passenger, never as transport and never as a driver.

On foot, the pet walks beside the trainer or rides in the trainer satchel if the road is rough. On a horse, in a carriage, in a gig, in a trail cart, in a freight wagon, in an armored carriage, or in a horse buggy, the pet rides at the trainer feet or in the trainer lap. On a boat or a kayak, the pet sits in the boat, dry and safe. On a Sky Skiff, the pet rides with the trainer between towns in one world. On a Sky Galleon, the pet stays in the cabin with the trainer on inter-world voyages.

A pet needs no profession license to travel. Pilot, Sailor, Rider, and Coachman licenses belong to Mankind, and a pet never needs one. A pet pays no fare, no fuel cost, and no docking fee. A pet never drives, never steers, never rows, and never flies a ship.

A pet is too small to ride. A trainer never rides a pet. Riding belongs to horses, Golden Striders, and transport, never to pets. A pet never carries the trainer, never pulls a cart, and never pulls a carriage.

If the trainer leaves a pet at the homestead before travel, the pet waits at the homestead, safe and fed from the homestead store, until the trainer returns. Travel never harms a pet, and a pet never gets left behind by accident: the game asks the trainer to pick the Active Pet or leave all pets at home before a long voyage.

### Thirteen Crowns card game

#### 1. The name and the idea

Thirteen Crowns is a full card game inside FED-13, not a side toy and not a short diversion. It is played on tavern tables and guild hall tables across all 13 worlds, with painted cards on stiff parchment kept in a leather case. The name comes from the match itself: a match lasts up to 13 tricks, and the first player to win 7 tricks takes the crown for that match. The game is easy to start and hard to master. You learn it by playing it. You lose a few tricks, you see why you lost, you change one choice, and you win the next trick. That is the whole teaching method.

#### 2. What you need to play

You need a deck of 30 cards and an opponent with a deck of 30 cards. You need a flat table. That is all. There is no board, no dice, and no separate currency inside a match. Your deck is yours. You build it, you keep it, and you carry it from world to world in your pack. A match takes about five minutes once you know the flow, and about ten minutes the first few times while the game shows you each new rule as it comes up.

#### 3. The cards

There are 143 cards in the v1 pool, in three types.

Ally cards: 100 cards in the pool. An Ally card shows a Bondkin or a Beast. Each Ally card has one element and one Strength number from 1 to 9. Ally cards are the heart of every deck. Most tricks are won and lost with Ally cards. Examples: a Puffkin Ally with Strength 4 and the Wind element, a Drakescale Ally with Strength 8 and the Fire element, a Slime Beast with Strength 2 and the Water element.

Hero cards: 30 cards in the pool. A Hero card shows a Mankind hero, a guild master, a sailor, a scholar, a warden, or a Trial Battle leader you have already beaten. Each Hero card has one element and a Strength number from 7 to 9, plus one short skill written on the card in plain words. A Hero card is strong, but a Hero card is rare in a deck, so you must pick the right trick to play it in. Examples of Hero skills: Rally, which lets you draw one extra card if you lose the trick; Guard, which blocks the other player's Crown card effect for that trick; Swift, which lets you draw one extra card at the start of the next round.

Crown cards: 13 cards in the pool, one for each world. A Crown card has Strength 5 and one strong effect written on the card in plain words. A Crown card can turn a lost trick into a won trick, but each Crown card can be used only once per match, and you can hold at most 3 Crown cards in a deck. Examples: Crown of Saltquay, win this trick even if your Strength is lower; Crown of Forestgate, swap one card in your hand with the top card of your deck; Crown of Canoebay, both players reveal their next card early, then you pick whether to play it or keep it.

Every card shows its art, its name, its element, its Strength, and its skill if it has one. Nothing is hidden on a card. If a card has a skill, the skill text is the rule, and it works exactly as written.

#### 4. The elements and the advantage ring

Every card has one of seven elements: Earth, Water, Fire, Wind, Time, Space, or Mirage. The elements form two rings, and the rings do not mix.

Ring of Four: Fire beats Wind, Wind beats Earth, Earth beats Water, Water beats Fire. If your element beats the other player's element, add 3 to your Strength for that trick only.

Ring of Three: Time beats Space, Space beats Mirage, Mirage beats Time. If your element beats the other player's element, add 3 to your Strength for that trick only.

If both cards are from different rings, neither element beats the other, and no bonus applies. If both cards have the same element, no bonus applies. The bonus is always plus 3, never more, and it applies only to the trick being played.

You do not need to memorize the rings before your first match. The game shows the ring on the table the first time an advantage matters, and it marks the winning element with a small crown sign. After three or four matches, most players know both rings by heart.

#### 5. How a match is played, step by step

Step 1, shuffle and draw. Both players shuffle their 30 card decks. Both players draw 5 cards into their hand. The rest of the deck stays face down as the draw pile.

Step 2, pick a card. Each round, both players pick one card from their hand and lay it face down on the table. You pick at the same time as your opponent. You cannot see their pick, and they cannot see yours.

Step 3, reveal. Both cards turn face up at the same time. The game reads the Strength of each card, adds any element bonus, and shows both totals on the table so you can see the math.

Step 4, resolve skills. If either card has a skill, the skill happens now, in the order written on the cards. Crown card effects happen first, then Hero card skills. If a skill changes the result, the game shows the change before the winner is named.

Step 5, name the winner of the trick. The higher total wins the trick. The winner takes both cards into a score pile beside the table. The winner also scores 1 Crown point for that trick.

Step 6, draw. Both players draw 1 card from their draw pile, so each hand goes back to 5 cards. If a skill told you to draw an extra card, you draw it now.

Step 7, next round. Play another round the same way. A match runs for up to 13 rounds, one trick per round.

Step 8, end. The match ends as soon as one player reaches 7 Crown points, or when 13 rounds have been played. If the draw pile runs out before 13 rounds, keep playing with the cards in hand until one player reaches 7 Crown points or the hands are empty.

#### 6. How you win, and what happens on a tie

First to 7 Crown points wins the match. If all 13 rounds are played and neither player has 7 points, the player with more Crown points wins. If the score is tied after 13 rounds, or if both hands are empty with a tied score, the match goes to sudden death: each player draws 1 card from the top of their draw pile, or from their score pile if the draw pile is empty, and the higher Strength wins the whole match. Element bonuses still apply in sudden death. Crown card effects do not apply in sudden death, so the last trick is a plain test of Strength and element.

If both cards tie on a single trick, neither player scores. Both cards stay in the middle of the table, face up, and the winner of the next trick takes those cards as well as the next trick's cards, and scores 1 Crown point for the tied trick plus 1 for the new trick. A tie never wastes a round. It raises the stakes of the next round.

#### 7. How the game teaches you while you play

There is no long tutorial and no rule book to read before your first match. The game teaches one rule at a time, at the moment that rule matters.

Your first match is against Tib Brightpom, a Puffkin shopkeeper at the Saltquay guild hall. Pip tells you only three things before you start: play a card, higher Strength wins the trick, first to 7 tricks wins the match. Then you play. The element ring is not explained until the first trick where an element bonus changes the winner, and then Pip points at the ring on the table and says one line about it. Hero skills are not explained until you first play a Hero card, and then the game reads the skill aloud for you. Crown cards are not explained until you first draw one, and then Pip tells you to save it for a trick you would otherwise lose.

After every match, your Grimoire writes one short tip based on what just happened. If you lost three tricks to the element bonus, the tip names the ring you missed. If you played a Hero card too early, the tip says to hold it for a later trick. If you won, the tip names the choice that won it for you. Tips are one line each, and you can turn them off at any card table once you feel ready.

There is a practice table at every guild hall. Practice matches cost nothing, pay nothing, and do not count toward your rank. You can play practice matches as often as you like, against a patient Puffkin dealer who plays slowly and lets you take back a card once per match if you change your mind. Losing a practice match teaches the same lessons as losing a real match, with no cost.

#### 8. Where you play

Card tables sit in the three guild halls: Saltquay on the Realm Map, Forestgate in the fairy tale kingdom, and Canoebay on the ocean island. Each guild hall has a card room with four tables, a notice board with the weekly tourney list, and a card trader who swaps duplicate cards.

Card tables also sit in the tavern of every town in all 13 worlds, so you can play a quick match wherever you stop for the night. Travelling card players walk the roads between towns and will challenge you on the spot. Beating a travelling player unlocks that player as a Grimoire contact, so you can call them for a rematch later.

You can also play at your homestead, once you place a card table there. A homestead table lets you invite any Grimoire contact for a match, and lets you practice against your own Bondkin partner, who plays a simple deck and never keeps score.

#### 9. Who you play against

Opponents have four ranks. The rank sets the prize, the strength of the deck, and the skill of the play.

Table Novice: new players and friendly townsfolk. Decks are mostly Ally cards, Strength 1 to 5, with no Crown cards. Good for learning. Prize for a win: 50 Cr.

Table Journeyman: guild regulars, sailors, traders, and guards. Decks mix Ally cards and one Hero card, Strength up to 7, with one Crown card at most. Prize for a win: 100 Cr.

Table Master: guild champions, scholars, and Trial Battle leaders you have already beaten in battle. Decks are full 30 card builds with Hero cards and Crown cards, Strength up to 9, and planned tricks. Prize for a win: 200 Cr.

Crownkeeper: the best player in each guild hall, and the final champions of the Crown of Thirteen. Decks are the strongest in the game, with rare Hero cards and all three Crown slots filled. Prize for a win: 500 Cr, plus a rare card pick.

Named opponents, by guild hall:

Saltquay guild hall: Tib Brightpom, Puffkin shopkeeper, Novice, teaches the first match. Old Tomas, Mankind sailor, Journeyman, plays a Water deck. Brina Saltwright, Mankind harbor clerk, Master, plays a Fire deck. Maren Saltwright, the Realm Map Trial Battle leader, Master, plays a mixed deck once you have beaten her Trial Battle.

Forestgate guild hall: Wren Appleford, Harefolk herald, Novice, plays a Wind deck. Elder Moss, Elderkin lore keeper, Journeyman, plays a Time deck. Thornwick Rose, Hybrid ranger, Master, plays an Earth deck. Rosalind Thornwick, the fairy tale kingdom Trial Battle leader, Master, plays a Mirage deck once you have beaten her Trial Battle.

Canoebay guild hall: Kai Wavecrest, the ocean island Trial Battle leader, Master, plays a Water deck once you have beaten his Trial Battle. Lina Palebranch, Harefolk lookout, Journeyman, plays a Space deck. Nib Holloway, Puffkin navigator, Journeyman, plays a Wind deck. Tidereach Finn, Mankind fisher, Novice, plays a Water deck.

More opponents appear as you travel: a Drakescale guard in the lion kingdom savanna, a Furmask smith in the hidden tech kingdom, a Mudsnout trader in the underwater kingdom, and a Fatewardens watcher in the haunted house. Each new world adds at least one new Novice, one Journeyman, and one Master to the card tables.

#### 10. Tournaments

Weekly Hall Tourney: every week, each guild hall runs an 8 player single elimination tourney. Entry is 100 Cr. Win three matches in a row to take the hall title for that week. Prize: 500 Cr, one booster pack, and a card pick from the beaten finalist's deck. The hall champion stays on the notice board until next week.

Monthly Grand Crown: once a month, the three guild halls take turns hosting a 16 player tourney. Entry is 500 Cr. Win four matches in a row to take the Grand Crown. Prize: 3,000 Cr, three booster packs, and one rare Hero card that cannot be bought in any shop. Winning a Grand Crown raises your card rank by one step.

Crown of Thirteen: the endgame tourney, open only after you have beaten all 13 Trial Battles. Thirteen champions wait, one for each world, each with a deck built around that world's Crown card. Beat all thirteen in a row, with no healing of your deck between matches except a fresh draw, to earn the title Crownkeeper of Thirteen and the 13th Crown card, Crown of Gravewick. This is the hardest card challenge in the game, and it is meant to be attempted more than once.

#### 11. How you collect cards

Starter deck: Tib Brightpom gives you a starter deck of 30 common Ally cards free at the Saltquay guild hall, the first time you sit at a card table. The starter deck has no Hero cards and no Crown cards. It is enough to win Novice matches and start collecting.

Booster packs: Puffkin shops sell a booster pack of 5 cards for 100 Cr. A pack holds 4 Ally cards and 1 Hero or Crown card. Shop tier sets the pack: Tier 1 packs hold common cards, Tier 2 packs hold uncommon cards after you clear 4 disconnected worlds, Tier 3 packs hold rare cards after you clear 8 disconnected worlds.

First win card pick: the first time you beat any named opponent, you may pick 1 card from that opponent's deck to keep. You keep the card even if you lose the rematch. This is the fastest way to learn new decks, because you take the card that beat you.

Trade Shop: the Trade Shop in each main town takes 3 duplicate cards and gives 1 booster pack in return. Duplicate Hero cards trade 1 for 1 for a booster pack. Duplicate Crown cards trade 1 for 1 for a rare Hero card of your choice from the trader's list.

Chests and rewards: card packs hide in treasure chests in dungeons, and dispatch missions sometimes pay in card packs instead of Cr. Tournament prizes pay in packs and rare cards, as listed above.

#### 12. Deck building rules

A deck holds exactly 30 cards. At least 20 cards must be Ally cards. At most 2 cards may be Hero cards. At most 3 cards may be Crown cards. You may hold at most 3 copies of any one Ally card, at most 1 copy of any one Hero card, and at most 1 copy of any one Crown card. You may change your deck at any card table, at any Puffkin shop, or at your homestead table, for free, as often as you like. There is no cost to rebuild. A deck that breaks these rules cannot be used in a match or a tourney, and the table will mark the rule it breaks so you can fix it.

#### 13. Rewards and ranks

Crests: every win pays Cr, by opponent rank: Novice 50 Cr, Journeyman 100 Cr, Master 200 Cr, Crownkeeper 500 Cr. Practice matches pay nothing. Losing a match pays nothing, and costs nothing except the entry fee in a tourney.

Cards: first win card picks, booster packs, Trade Shop trades, chests, dispatch rewards, and tourney prizes, as listed above. No card in the game is locked behind a purchase with real money. Every card is earned by playing.

Titles and card rank: your card rank rises as you win. Ranks in order: Card Novice, Card Journeyman, Card Master, Crownkeeper, Grand Crown, Crownkeeper of Thirteen. Each rank unlocks stronger opponents, rarer packs in shops, and new tables. The title shows beside your name on the guild hall notice board, and in your Grimoire contacts list.

Special rewards: winning a Grand Crown gives a rare Hero card. Winning the Crown of Thirteen gives the Crown of Gravewick card and the Crownkeeper of Thirteen title. Beating all named opponents in all three guild halls gives the Golden Case, a leather deck case that holds 60 cards instead of 30, so you can carry two decks at once.

#### 14. How it fits FED-13

Thirteen Crowns fits the period setting: painted parchment cards, leather cases, tavern tables, guild hall card rooms, and travelling players on the roads. No modern tech is used to play it. It fits the economy: Crests pay for packs and tourney entry, and wins pay Crests back. It fits the worlds: each world has its own Crown card, its own champion, and its own card table, so collecting cards is a reason to travel. It fits the guild halls: the three guild halls are the home of the game, with the Saltquay hall as the place where every player learns it. It fits the Grimoire: your Grimoire records your wins, your rank, your contacts for rematches, and one line of advice after every match. It fits the Trial Battles: beating a Trial Battle leader unlocks that leader as a card opponent, so the card game grows as the main story grows. And it fits the learning rule of the whole game: you learn Thirteen Crowns by playing Thirteen Crowns, one trick at a time, until you know what to do.

#### Trigger

1. The Beast must hold its own Apex Shard. Each of the 14 Beasts has one named Shard, for example Dragon Shard for Dragon and Phoenix Shard for Phoenix. A Shard only works for its named Beast.
2. The Beast must be level 50 or higher.
3. The Mankind trainer uses Apex Call as the Beast's action for that turn. Apex Call is a battle command, not an Art, not a skill, and not a weapon.
4. Only one Beast per party can take Apex form in a single battle. Only one Apex Call can be used per battle. A Beast that has already taken Apex form in a battle cannot take Apex form again in that battle.
5. A fused Beast cannot take Apex form. A Beast cannot fuse in the same battle in which it has taken Apex form.
6. A Hollow Beast stays Hollow in Apex form. An Echo Beast stays Echo in Apex form. The variant does not change.

#### What changes

Look: every Apex form is larger, brighter, and more ornate than the base Beast, with new horns, wings, plates, flames, or light, as described per Beast below and in Part B. The base colors stay recognizable in every view.

Type: the base type stays, and one type is added, or one type is replaced, as listed per Beast below. Type changes apply only while the form lasts.

Stats: every Apex form gains plus 100 total stats, split as HP plus 10, plus 30 to each of its two best attacking or defending stats, plus 10 to SPD, plus 10 to the remaining defense stat, plus 10 to the remaining attack stat. The exact split per Beast is listed below. No stat goes above the normal cap for its level.

Moves: every Apex form gains one signature Beast move, usable only while the form lasts. The signature move replaces one of the Beast's four set moves for that battle. The Beast keeps its other three set moves. The signature move is a Beast move only. It is not an Art, not a skill, and not a weapon move. When the form ends, the replaced move returns.

No Arts, no skills, no weapons: Beasts cannot use Arts or skills and cannot use weapons, and Apex Form does not change that. An Apex form uses only Beast moves. It gains no job, no class, and no equipment slot.

#### Duration

The Apex form lasts until the battle ends, or until the Beast faints, or until the Beast is withdrawn from battle. When the form ends, the Beast returns to its base form at the same HP percentage it had in Apex form. Status conditions stay. The signature move is lost when the form ends. Experience gained while in Apex form is kept.

#### Cost and items

Apex Shard: one per Beast, held item, never consumed by taking Apex form. Earned from boss chests, Trial Battle rewards, and late game synthesis at a Puffkin shop. A Shard cannot be bought at a Tier 1 Puffkin shop.

Attunement: a Shard must be attuned once before first use. Attunement costs 500 Crests at any Puffkin shop and is done once per Beast. After attunement, Apex Call costs no Crests in battle.

Apex Call: free in battle, limited to once per battle per party, as above. There is no per use item cost after attunement.

#### Rules that stay fixed

Apex Form is a temporary battle form, not an evolution. It does not change the Codex number, the species name, or the Bank slot. A Beast that takes Apex form keeps its level, its experience, and its held Shard. A Beast cannot take Apex form in the wild. Only a trained Beast in a party can take Apex form. A Beast that becomes a Bondkin keeps its Apex Shard and can take Apex form as a Bondkin in battle, with the same limits.
